Design Week 02 — Micro Mix Game

A small arcade-style game built around a single verb, developed through rapid sketching, iteration, and a console-style compliancy check.

For Design Week 02, the challenge was to create a micro mix game — a tiny arcade experience built entirely around one verb. Before choosing the verb, we first explored different game ideas. I pitched a cooking concept where the player slices vegetables directly inside a pot, focusing on rhythm, timing, and tactile feedback.

After reviewing all the ideas, we compared which concepts had the clearest core action. Only then did we choose the verb. The team ultimately selected fight, which led us toward a fighting rhythm game where timing and impact were the heart of the experience.

My main responsibility was creating the game’s background. Even though the gameplay itself was 2D, I built the environment in 3D to give us more control over lighting, depth, and atmosphere. This approach helped the visuals feel more dynamic while still supporting the clarity required for a micro mix game.

The game also needed to pass a compliancy check, modeled after the certification process developers go through before releasing a game on major consoles. This meant ensuring the build met technical and usability standards: readable UI, consistent controls, no soft-locks, and stable performance.

Key Lessons

  • Pitching Ideas. Presenting my cooking concept helped clarify what makes a strong single-verb game.
  • Verb Selection. Choosing the verb after choosing the game direction created a more natural design flow.
  • Compliancy Thinking. Treating the project like a console submission improved polish and discipline.
  • Scope Awareness. Micro games demand clarity — every detail must support the core verb.

Visual Direction

  • 3D-to-2D Workflow. I built a 3D environment to support a 2D gameplay loop.
  • Lighting Control. Using 3D allowed for stronger mood and readability.
  • Supporting the Verb. The background needed to enhance the rhythm and impact of “fight” without distracting from gameplay.

Iteration

  • Sketching. I created multiple environment sketches before development began.
  • Feedback Loops. Each day began with critique and refinement.
  • Refining the Concept. The fighting rhythm direction emerged through iteration, not from the initial pitch.
  • Background Polish. Adjusted lighting, depth, and silhouettes to support gameplay clarity.

Process Notes

  • Early Concepts. Explored multiple game ideas before settling on the final direction.
  • Verb Selection. The verb “fight” was chosen only after the team aligned on the game’s tone.
  • 3D Environment. Built a stylized 3D background for a 2D game to enhance atmosphere.
  • Compliancy Checklist. Ensured the game met submission-style requirements.

Takeaway

This project taught me how much clarity and intention matter when designing around a single verb. It also reinforced the value of iteration — from pitching ideas, to refining sketches, to polishing the final environment. Working through a console-style compliancy check gave me a deeper appreciation for the technical and usability standards behind even the smallest games.
