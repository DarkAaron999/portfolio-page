Design Week 03 — A Short Hike Board Game Adaptation

A physical redesign of the video game A Short Hike, transformed into a cooperative analog board game with exploration, discovery, and progression at its core.

Design Challenge: Adapt an existing digital game into a fully analog, non-digital experience with a physical component. My team was assigned A Short Hike, a game I already loved for its cozy exploration, gentle progression, and sense of freedom.

We began by breaking down the original game’s core loop: explore → collect → get feathers → climb. Personally, I connected most with the fishing and gliding mechanics — they made the world feel alive and rewarding.

Minimum Viable Scenarios

  • Flying and gliding
  • Freedom of choice
  • Climbing the mountain

Essential Ingredients

  • Feather collection
  • Mountain progression
  • Side objectives and quests
  • Tools such as shovel, pickaxe, and bucket
  • A sense of expansion and discovery

Non-Essentials

  • Music and racing
  • Currency
  • Decorative aesthetics

Concepting the Board Game

I proposed several analog inspirations to help shape the board game:

  • Catan — modular board structure
  • Snakes and Ladders — vertical progression
  • Battleship — fog of war

Team Vision

  • A fixed board shape with layered elevation
  • Face-down cards placed on marked spaces
  • Cards flip when passed or landed on
  • 3-player cooperative gameplay
  • Completed challenges become easier to traverse
  • Tunnels act as mastery shortcuts
  • Players can meet anywhere to trade items
  • All players must reach the summit to win

Playtesting

Our first prototype surfaced several issues:

  • The Miner role felt underpowered
  • Tunnels weren’t being used
  • The board was too small for the number of tokens

Despite these challenges, we had meaningful successes:

  • Players felt encouraged to explore
  • Movement felt meaningful
  • The emotional tone of the original game came through
  • NPC quests were funny and engaging
  • Teamwork emerged naturally

Improvements

After refining the board and rulebook, the second playtest revealed new issues but also clear progress.

  • Rulebook required syncing
  • Roles still needed balancing
  • Diagrams were missing

But we also saw strong improvements:

  • Movement became intuitive
  • Players cooperated naturally
  • NPC dialogue added personality
  • Classes felt distinct

My Contributions

  • Designed icons for pond, rock, tunnel, feather, villager, and item tokens
  • Coloured the Miner, Fisher, and Digger characters
  • Cut and prepared tokens for the final version

Takeaway

This project taught me how to translate a digital experience into a physical one without losing its emotional core. Seeing players laugh, strategize, and explore together made the process incredibly rewarding.
