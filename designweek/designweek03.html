Design Week 03 — A Short Hike Board Game Adaptation

A physical redesign of the video game A Short Hike, transformed into a cooperative analog board game with exploration, discovery, and progression at its core.

Design Challenge: Adapt an existing digital game into a fully analog, non-digital experience with a physical component. My team was assigned A Short Hike, a game I already loved for its cozy exploration, gentle progression, and sense of freedom. I was excited to translate its emotional core into something tactile, collaborative, and replayable.

We began by breaking down the original game’s core loop: explore → collect → get feathers → climb. Personally, I connected most with the fishing and gliding mechanics — they made the world feel alive and rewarding. The game also includes trading, currency, races, and a charming narrative, but we focused on what was essential to the experience.

Minimum Viable Scenarios

  • Flying and gliding
  • Freedom of choice
  • Climbing the mountain

Essential Ingredients

  • Feather collection
  • Mountain progression
  • Side objectives and quests
  • Tools such as shovel, pickaxe, and bucket
  • A sense of expansion and discovery

Non-Essentials

  • Music and racing
  • Currency
  • Decorative aesthetics

Concepting the Board Game

I proposed several analog inspirations to help shape the board game:

  • Catan — for modular or player-built board structure
  • Snakes and Ladders — for vertical progression up the mountain
  • Battleship — for fog of war and hidden information

I also sketched two board layout concepts to explore how players might uncover the world as they move through it. The goal was to preserve the feeling of discovery that defines A Short Hike.

Team Vision

After combining everyone’s proposals, we aligned on a unified direction:

  • A fixed board shape with layered elevation
  • Face-down cards placed on marked spaces
  • Cards flip when passed or landed on
  • 3-player cooperative gameplay
  • Completed challenges become easier to traverse
  • Tunnels act as mastery shortcuts
  • Players can meet anywhere to trade items
  • All players must reach the summit to win

Playtesting and Iteration

Our first prototype was simple but functional. During playtesting, we discovered several issues:

  • The Miner role felt underpowered
  • Tunnels weren’t being used
  • The board was too small for the number of tokens

Despite these challenges, we had meaningful successes:

  • Players felt encouraged to explore
  • Movement felt meaningful and intentional
  • The emotional tone of the original game came through
  • NPC quests were funny and engaging
  • Teamwork emerged naturally

Improvements

After reviewing feedback from the first playtest, we refined the board, clarified the rules, and rebalanced the roles. The second unguided playtest surfaced new issues but also showed clear progress.

  • Rulebook required syncing across sections
  • Roles still needed balancing
  • Diagrams were missing and needed to be added

But we also saw strong improvements:

  • Movement became intuitive and easy to understand
  • Players cooperated naturally to complete quests
  • NPC dialogue was engaging and added personality
  • Classes felt distinct and meaningful

My Contributions

I took on a large portion of the visual and physical design work:

  • Designed icons for pond, rock, tunnel, feather, villager, and item tokens
  • Coloured the character illustrations for Miner, Fisher, and Digger
  • Cut and prepared tokens for the final version

Takeaway

This project taught me how to translate a digital experience into a physical one without losing its emotional core. Balancing mechanics, clarity, and theme was challenging, but seeing players laugh, strategize, and explore together made the process incredibly rewarding. It reinforced how much thoughtful iteration, playtesting, and visual design contribute to a cohesive player experience.
