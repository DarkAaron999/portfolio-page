Design Week 03 — A Short Hike Board Game Adaptation

This week’s challenge was to take a digital game and reimagine it as a physical, analog experience. My team was assigned A Short Hike, a cozy exploration game I already loved for its sense of freedom, progression, and charm. I was excited to translate its emotional core into something tactile and collaborative.

We started by identifying the game’s core loop: explore, collect, get feathers, climb. I personally connected with the fishing and gliding mechanics — they made the world feel alive and rewarding. The game also had trading, currency, races, and a strong aesthetic, but we focused on what was essential to the experience.

We broke the game down into three tiers:

Minimum Viable Scenarios

  • Flying/gliding
  • Freedom of choice
  • Climbing the mountain

Essential Ingredients

  • Feather collection
  • Mountain progression
  • Side objectives
  • Items like shovel, pickaxe, bucket
  • A sense of expansion and discovery

Non-Essentials

  • Music and racing
  • Currency
  • Decorative aesthetics

Concepting the Board Game

I pitched ideas based on familiar analog games:

  • Catan for player-built board structure
  • Snakes and Ladders for vertical progression
  • Battleship for fog of war and hidden information

I also sketched two board layouts to visualize how exploration and discovery could work physically. The goal was to preserve the feeling of uncovering the world as you move through it.

Team Vision

We merged our ideas into a single concept:

  • Fixed board shape with layered elevation
  • Cards placed face-down on marked spots
  • Cards flip when passed or landed on
  • 3-player co-op
  • Challenges become easier once completed
  • Tunnels represent mastery and shortcuts
  • Players can meet anywhere to trade items
  • All players must reach the summit to win

Playtesting and Iteration

Our first prototype was simple but functional. During playtesting, we noticed some issues:

  • The Miner role felt underpowered
  • Tunnels weren’t being used
  • The board was too small for the number of tokens

Despite that, we had some real wins:

  • Players felt encouraged to explore
  • Movement felt meaningful
  • The emotional tone of the original game came through
  • NPC quests were funny and engaging
  • Teamwork happened naturally

We ran a second, unguided playtest with a refined board and rulebook. This surfaced new challenges:

  • Rulebook needed syncing
  • Roles weren’t fully balanced
  • Diagrams were missing

But we also saw improvements:

  • Movement was intuitive
  • Players cooperated to complete quests
  • NPC dialogue was engaging
  • Classes felt distinct and special

My Contributions

I took on a lot of the visual and physical design work:

  • Created icons for pond, rock, tunnel, feather, villager, and item tokens
  • Colored the character illustrations for Miner, Fisher, and Digger
  • Helped cut and prepare tokens for the final version

This project taught me how to distill a digital experience into something physical without losing its soul. It was a challenge to balance mechanics, emotion, and clarity — but seeing players laugh, strategize, and explore together made it worth it.
